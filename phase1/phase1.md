# 1 — Run-day security process (end-to-end) Walk me through a typical session from the moment the group meets to the regroup at the bottom. Specifically:

## Pre-run: 

brief content, role assignment, rider order/departure spacing, gear check sequence.

## During run: 

opener car position relative to the first rider, how far apart riders ride, speed control mechanism, what happens when the spotter spots something.

## Communication rhythm: 

the talkie cadence — who talks, when, how often, what triggers a call.

## Post-run: 

debrief? anything logged?

# 2 — Spotter's exact job in the car

## What's the spotter's vantage point? Height, position in the car, speed of the car vs riders?

## What can the spotter actually see (road ahead, oncoming, hidden accesses, pedestrians)?

## Decision tree: when the spotter sees something, what does he say and in what order? Is there a priority (car > motorbike > pedestrian > dog)?

## Does the spotter have a co-pilot or is he alone?

## What does the spotter not see that a rider on the road would?

# 3 — The sign-based workaround (how the deaf rider is kept in the loop today)

## Who gives the signs? Just one designated rider, or anyone nearby?

## What do the signs look like (hand positions, gestures, number of fingers)?

## Range: how far ahead / behind can signs still be read? Does it work through curves?

## Failure modes of the sign system: what breaks it (speed, distance, nobody in front, hairpin turn, sun glare)?

## How does the deaf rider signal back to the group (acknowledgment, alarm)?

# 4 — Deaf rider's current experience

## On a typical run, how often does he have NO information (no sign, no talkie)?

## What does that feel like? Specific anxiety moments, or just general caution?

## Has he ever ridden with a prototype or partial solution? What worked / broke?

# 5 — Failure cases / near-misses (more detail) For the 3 you already listed, I have the triggers. I need:

## What was the actual outcome each time?

## What was the immediate response?

## What was learned and changed afterward (if anything)?

# 6 — Environment & group

## Typical group size (how many riders total, how many deaf)?

## Road profile: width, surface quality, typical hairpin frequency, visibility range.

## Weather: do you ride in rain? Does it change the process?

## Is there ever a "lead rider" ahead of the spotter, or is the spotter always first?

# 7 — Usability / proportionality (design constraints for phase 3–4)

## For non-deaf riders: what's the absolute maximum gear change they'd accept without complaint? (e.g. "wearing a second wrist band" vs "replacing the talkie" — where's the ceiling?)

## For the spotter: what's the maximum cognitive load while driving? One extra button press per call? A foot pedal? A voice command?

## For the deaf rider: is a helmet-mounted display acceptable, or must it be glove-only (tactile)? Or is the answer "either, let's see"?
